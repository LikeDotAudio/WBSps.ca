The M7880H is a simplified sixteen key communications terminal. Providing simplified operational characteristics where the full capability of the M7880D terminal is not required.

This terminal is self powered, containing the required audio amplifiers and microprocessor based control system, to operate all control and communications paths to and from the central switching matrix. It is inclusive of an integral dynamic gooseneck microphone, loudspeaker and sixteen talk select keyswitches.

The control, assignment and programming of keyswitch data is transferred to and from the central matrix on a balanced serial RS422 data line. The audio input and output of the M7880H are balanced 600 ohm lines operating at +8 or +4 dBu. The above three balanced lines allow full communications between the terminal and the central matrix.

The M7880H has sixteen programmable talk keyswitch functions, each consisting of a momentary and a lock pushbutton. The pushbutton pair replicates the traditional telephone lever key, with its centre off position, an up lock and a down momentary position. The lock key position is cancelled any time the momentary function is selected.

Associated with the sixteenth talk keyswitch is a Call Assign pushbutton. This keyswitch is reserved for answering unassigned calls to the communications station (Call Waiting). No assignment is accepted by this keyswitch from the central assignment computer.

Optionally the lock keyswitch tally may be programmed as an identifying calling tally.

Audio from the integral dynamic or the external headset microphone is amplified to a nominal line level of +8/+4 dBu. A front panel sensitivity control (SENS) is available to make level adjustments for variations in operating techniques. Input levels over a sensitivity range of 30 dB are accommodated.

An auto levelling type limiter stabilizes the audio output from the microphone preamplifier, assuring constant system levels over a large range of voice variations. Two level indicators, located above the sensitivity control are provided. One labelled NORM (normal), it flashes when the preamplifier output is a nominal +8/+4 VU. The other labelled OVLD (overload) flashes when the limiting action becomes excessive (greater than 20 dB).

A third indicator, labelled XMIT illuminates when an assigned talk keyswitch is operated, indicating audio is being transmitted from the communications terminal. An external footswitch control may be looped in series with the lock talk keyswitch control functions. 

A power amplifier boosts the incoming audio signal from the matrix and drives an integral speaker. A front panel level control is provided for the speaker output. The amplifier is capable of delivering up to 3 watts into an 8 ohm load.

The terminal is powered from an integral modular power supply, operating from 115/230 Volts AC 50/60 Hz. 

The M7880H terminal is housed in a 3-1/2 inch high (2RU) aluminum chassis, designed for standard nineteen inch rack mounting. Its shallow depth (less than 10 inches) allows the terminal to be easily mounted in typical television production desk operating areas. It is constructed on a modular philosophy, with all active circuit modules interconnected via ribbon cables and chassis type connectors. All modules are easily removed for any required service.

This manual section outlines the fea­tures and func­tions of the M7880H communi­cations terminal with operating system software "MTG" #50-715, rel. 1.01 or greater. The operating system soft­ware is installed in the SA14-270G controller assem­bly in EPROM U3.

1)	TALK KEYSWITCHES

Sixteen programmable talk keyswitch func­tions are provided, each with a momen­tary pushbutton and a latching pushbutton with an integral LED indica­tor. This keyswitch pair repli­cates the traditional telephone lever key. The lower momen­tary pushbutton acts as the momentary down stroke of the tele­phone lever key. The upper latching pushbutton acts as the up lock position of the lever key, the latch condition is tallied with a red LED indi­cator. Operating the lower momentary pushbutton will cancel the latch condi­tion of the upper push but­ton if it was set.

Fifteen of the talk keyswitches (labelled 1 to 15 are programmable from the cen­tral assignment com­puter. The sixteenth or Call Reply key with its asso­ci­ated Call Assign pushbutton is dedi­cated to answer­ing calls to the com­munications station, that are not assigned to the other keyswitches.

The talk keyswitches may be pro­grammed to talk to a number of stations simulta­neously. This can be done by the Group (GRP) func­tion associ­ated with each ter­minal or by the Call Array (CA) feature. Also they may be pro­grammed via the Event func­tions of the Elec­tronic Peg Board (EPC) to operate any crosspoints in the sys­tem on a co-ordinate basis.

The locking talk keyswitches loop through an external footswitch function that is access­ible on rear connector P2.

If this function is not used a bypass jumper must be installed on P2. As sup­plied a bypass jumper is installed inter­nal­ly on the SA14-270G controller assem­bly, see the installation section for its removal and implementation of an exter­nal footswitch control. The audio trans­mit output and the crosspoint control assigned to locking keyswitches are in series with the external footswitch con­trol.

Any time a talk keyswitch is operated and audio is transmitted from the ter­minal the speaker output is part muted. The degree of part muting is set via a rear panel trim con­trol (see item 16 on the rear panel). An open collec­tor tally control output is pro­vided for the momentary and for the lock talk keyswitches. These maybe used to operate external devices or control other terminal audio functions (eg. speaker mute).

TALK KEYSWITCH ASSIGNMENTS

The talk control functions for a M7880H terminal are defined in the central assignment computer and then sent to the appropriate terminal. This assignment information is stored and retained in the terminal connected to a particular matrix bus. Listen control attributes associated with any assignment code are ignored, the Talk control attribute must be set for the talk keyswitch function to operate.

In the assignment computer Matrix Index Table defi­ni­tion, select the M7880D ter­minal type for matrix buses utilizing the M7880H ter­minal.

The Group (GRP) assignments for matrix buses using the M7880H terminal may be defined and sent to the appropri­ate ter­minal. It will be necessary to pro­gram the desired keyswitch with phantom codes GRP1, GRP2, GRP3 or GRP4.

Other phantom code assignments that are applicable to the M7880H terminals are:

Call Arrays	CA01 to CA12.
Event Control	EV01 to EV24.


2)	DESIGNATION STRIP

Two designation strips are supplied. The lower is labelled as per the key number. These designation strips may be labelled as required for the application at hand.

3)	SENS

The sensitivity trim control provides adjustment of the microphone preamplifier output, to make up for vari­ations in operating technique. The sensi­tivity control may be operationally set by speaking into the microphone in a normal voice at the correct operating distance. Advance the control clockwise until the green NORM LED indicator begins flash­ing intermit­tently. DO NOT advance any further. Under no circum­stances should the con­trol be set so the red OVLD indi­cator flashes on a continuous basis (excess­ive limit­ing).

4)	NORM

The green Normal LED indicator flashes when the output level from the microphone preamplifier is a nominal +8/+4 VU.
5)	OVLD

The red LED Overload indicator flashes when the limiting action becomes excess­ive (greater than 20 dB).

6)	LINE

The yellow LED indicator illuminates when the communications terminal con­trol system is communicating with the central matrix.

7)	XMIT

The transmit indicator will illuminate when any of the momentary talk keyswitches are activated. Or when a lock talk keyswitch has been selected (red indi­cator illuminated) and the external footswitch closure is made. This indicator tells an operator that audio is being trans­mitted from the communications terminal to the matrix.

8)	MICROPHONE

The front panel mounted microphone is a dynamic, unidirectional type with a cardioid pickup pattern. The microphone offers high speech intelligibility and excel­lent feedback suppression.

9)	CALL ASSIGN & REPLY

The sixteenth talk keyswitch (D-key on other M7880 series terminals) is known as the Call Reply key. Associated with this talk keyswitch is the Call Assign pushbutton.

A call received from a station that is not assigned to the other fifteen keyswitches will cause the red LED indicator in the pushbutton to begin flashing. This is an indication that an unassigned call is wait­ing to be answered. It is to be noted that the audio from any station is heard by another regardless of the condition of key control assignments (no blocking of audio paths). 

To answer the unassigned incoming call press the Call Assign pushbutton, its indi­cator switches to green, indicating the call code is received and assigned to the Call Reply talk keyswitch below it. The oper­ator now may talk to the incoming call. To release the assignment press the Call Assign pushbutton a second time, the indicator will extinguish and the Call Reply talk keyswitch will be unassigned.

Only one call assignment is received at a time, it will be the last call in. The Call Waiting assignment indication will remain for approximately 1-1/2 minutes before it will cancel and extinguish the red call waiting indicator after the initial call is made. Once an unassigned call is selected (the pushbutton indicator is green) additional un-assigned calls will be ignored by the call waiting assignment and selection feature (the calling station audio will always be heard). The second call may be answered by releasing the current Call Waiting assignment (cancelling it out) and reselecting the new one, if it still persists.
Calling Tallies

The red LED tally indicators of the lock talk keyswitches may be programmed to serve as a call tally indicator. See the installation section and rear connector P2.

If this function is enabled the lock keyswitch tally indicator will flash when a call is received which is associated with the talk keyswitch in question. The indi­cator will flash for the duration of the calling station selection plus approxi­mately 4 seconds.

10)	SPEAKER AND LEVEL CONTROL

The level control provides adjustment of the received communications audio from the central matrix, which is fed to the front panel loudspeaker.

Power Requirements:

The M7880H Communications T­erminal is designed to operate directly from an AC power source with its integral modular power supply. The power supply is access­ible by remov­ing the top and bottom covers of the terminal.

Note: Service and adjustment of the power supply unit should only be per­formed by personnel knowledgeable with the tech­nology employed and with the correct isolated power sources and test equipment. High voltages are present and extreme caution must be taken.

The power supply may be internally strapped for 115V or 230V AC operation. The power con­sumpt­ion is nomi­nally 50 VA. Some power supplies accommodate an AC power source from 85V AC to 250V AC without the requirement of a voltage strap.

To gain access to the 115V/230 voltage strap refer to section E of this manual:

Note: the power supply will be damaged if jum­pered or strapped for 115 volts and oper­at­ed on 230V. 
Mounting

The M7880H is designed for stan­dard nine­teen inch rack mounting. It re­quires only 
two rack units of vertical space (3 1/2 inches) and about eleven inches of mount­ing depth with con­nectors in place. The unit weights 11 lbs (5 kg).

Matrix Connections:

The M7880H is connected to the inter­com matrix via three audio pairs such as Bel­den 8777 or 8451 cable, which extends from data connector P1 at the rear of the unit. Page 6-1 of this manual shows the layout for this connector. Insure that the phase of the serial data pair is correct when wiring this connec­tor (P1 pin 4 corre­sponds to the low numbered terminal on the matrix termi­nal block).

The typical ground and shielding connec­tions between a communications terminal and the central matrix are as follows:

a)	The shield of the data line must be car­ried through between the com­munications ter­minal ground and that of the central matrix (P1 pin 9, see page 6-1).
b)	The preamplifier output from the com­munications terminal (P1 pin 6) con­nects to the matrix ground at the matrix ter­minating connector.

c)	The speaker amplifier input or audio in from the matrix has its shield con­nected to the communications terminal ground at P1 pin 3.

All shields should be wired through all connectors to ensure proper and cor­rect shielding, particularly when extension cables are employed.
Configuration:

The following summarizes the M7880H terminal  configuration for operating system software MTG #50-715 revision 1.01 or greater.

1)	Terminal Type:	Set to M7880D in Matrix Index table definition of Central Assignment computer.

2)	Matrix size:	Preset to 96.

3)	Call Waiting:	Enabled for Call Reply key (D key).

4)	Privacy Override:	Momentary talk keyswitches are not programmed to override lock talk keyswitches.

5)	IFB lock key:	Enabled.

6)	Camera Iso:	Not selectable from this terminal.

7)	Expansion Panel:	Not assignable.

8)	Initialize:	Momentarily grounding P2 pin 5 will re-initialize the terminal as outlined above. All keyswitch and GRP (group) assignments will be lost. A terminal's keyswitch assignments and GRP (group) assignments will have to be initiated from the assignment computer.




For the MTG (#50-715) software to operate  the M7880H communications terminal it must be conditionally set. This is determined by the setting of the external control input at connector P2 pin 4.

As factory shipped a M7880H will have P2 pin 4 internally jumpered to control common (ground) at P2 pin 7 or 8.

Grounding this pin (P2-4) configures the operating system software to operate the M7880H terminal, while leaving it open (not grounded) configures the operating system for the M7880G/J terminal operation.

If an interfacing mating connector J2 is to be installed. It is recommended that the appropriate jumpers be connected to the required  pins of the mating connector.

To switch from one configuration to another it will be necessary to re-initialize the terminal operating system software. This occurs every time the power is removed and re-applied, or if external input P2 pin 5 is momentarily grounded.

Call Tallies:

To enable the tallies of the lock talk keyswitches to function as calling tallies ground P2 pin 6 to control common P2 pin 7. (refer to page 6-2 for connector layout detail).

With the lock talk keyswitch tally merged with the calling tally feature, operating ambi­guity or confusion may result. Particularly if calling station talk keyswitches are locked on for an extended period of time.

Audio Connections:

The M7880H may be operated with its inte­gral speaker and front panel microphone or with a headset. An external speaker may be connected at P6-13 & 12, after disconnecting the terminal speaker by cutting link LK5 on the SA14-271 audio assembly. The speaker interconnect at P6-25 may be used to reconnect the terminal speaker.

The headset connection is made via rear con­nector P6 of the M7880H terminal. A headset such as the Beyer DT108/DT109 series with a 400 ohm or greater headset impedance and an amplified dynamic microphone capsule is recommended. The phantom power required to operate the preamplifier circuit integral to the headset is supplied from the terminal. Optionally a dynamic microphone capsule type may be used, but additional care must be taken with interconnects due to the great level differ­ences between this type of micro­phone and the headset levels. 

The headset function is selected by connect­ing P6-22 to control common (P6-12). The front panel dynamic microphone is switched off, the headset microphone inputs to P6-1 & 2 are turned on and the front panel speaker is muted. Microphone sidetone is injected into the headset earpiece. Sidetone level adjust­ment is accessible on the rear of the M7880H Terminal. 

Connector P6 (page 6-3) provides the means of connecting an external headset from a second­ary receptacle into the M7880H terminal. Caution must be exercised when using an unbalanced headset. Connect the hot or high side of the ear piece to P6-11 and the common to P6-9. No connection is made to P6-10.

The figures on pages 4-6 to 4-7 illustrate suggested wiring for headsets to the communi­cations terminal. Note: Cable lengths should be limited to ten feet or less.

An external preamplifier input is provided at P6-15 & 16. This sound feed is separate from the microphone input. It is selected by con­necting P6-17 to control common (P6-12). 

Two switchable inputs are provided to the speaker amplifier. They appear at P6-5 & 6 and P6-7 & 8. Control of these inputs is via P6-18 & 19 to control common (P6-12).

Audio Levels:

The system audio levels can be set for +4 or +8 dBu operation. To set the preamplifier output level refer to the SA14-271 audio assembly link 9 and note 7. The receive speaker amplifier input level is calibrated by the adjustment of R116. With +4 or +8 dBu applied to the speaker amplifier input (audio from matrix), adjust R116 for 3 watts output, 3.9 volts rms at P6-13 with front panel speaker level control full cw.

Control Connections:

a)	An external footswitch control function may be used with the lock talk keyswitches. This allows the lock talk keyswitches to be preselected and then with the external footswitch control to be activated. The internal bypass link on the SA14-270G controller assembly must be removed and mating connector J2 installed. See configuration jumpering for J2 below.
b)	For applications where the microphone output must be on continuously, regard­less of keyswitch assignments and oper­ation, connect P6-20 to control common (P6-12). This is sometimes referred to as the 'Hot Mic Mode'.

c)	Part muting of the M7880H terminal speaker is integral to the system and is under software control. Any time a momentary talk keyswitch or a lock talk keyswitch via the footswitch link is oper­ated, the front panel speaker is partially muted. The degree of part mute is deter­mined by the adjust­ment of R120 (on the SA14-271 assembly) which is accessible at the rear of the M7880H terminal.

An external part mute control input is available at P6-23.

d)	Full muting of the terminal speaker may be implemented by connecting P6-24 to con­trol common (P6-12).
e)	Momentary and lock talk keyswitch oper­ation tallies are provided at P2-1 & 2 respectively. These open collector outputs may be jumpered to P6 for example, to implement a full speaker mute with the operation of a talk keyswitch.
