Quad Twisted Pair

Four balanced pairs in one jacket. QTP was Ward-Beck's series of cabling and connecting products: RJ45 and DB25 boards, a QTP version of the POD8A, an XLR encoder, a patchbay, and the RTO-144 that puts a classic Ward-Beck module on two RJ45 jacks.

6.1.1 Cold open: four pairs in one jacket

VO Look inside a network cable and you find four twisted pairs. Ethernet uses them for data. But a twisted pair is also exactly what a balanced audio line has always been. Ward-Beck saw a cable that was cheap, everywhere and already in the walls, and four audio circuits in every run. It called the idea QTP: quad twisted pair.

6.1.2 The QTP series [ARCHIVIST: first QTP product and year]

VO QTP was never one product. The manuals call it a series of cabling and connecting products: small boards and panels that move audio between the connectors a studio already has and the four pairs of an RJ45. The parts list has a whole group of them. The QTP-DB25 boards come in male, female and Y versions, and in July 2015 the inventory counted seventy in one bin.

6.1.3 The POD8A goes QTP

VO The POD8A, a distribution amplifier from 2015, came with a choice of rear panels: terminal blocks, XLRs, or QTP. Same amplifier inside, and the outputs leave on twisted-pair cable instead of a loom of individual lines.

6.1.4 From the XLR

VO Most microphones and most patch points still end in an XLR. The QTP-BAC encoder board, revision 17.1, drawn in April 2016, is where an XLR meets the pairs. The connector drawings in the parts library are marked QTP too: a Neutrik female and male, chosen for the job.

6.1.5 Sun, sky, grass and earth [ARCHIVIST: confirm the colour mapping]

VO Four pairs need four names you can say across a room. On the QTP patchbay drawing they are sun, sky, grass and earth, each with a top and a bottom, a plus and a minus. Orange, blue, green and brown: the colours of the cable's own pairs, so an engineer can trace a signal without a pinout card.

6.1.6 The meters join the series

VO By 2018 the rest of the catalogue was being redesigned around it. The AMS8-1 MKIII monitor and the MLC8A level controller moved their analog outputs to a DB25 with the Tascam pinout, and the manuals say why: it is now compatible with Ward-Beck's QTP series. One DB25, eight channels, two RJ45s away from the rest of the plant.

6.1.7 RTO-144: the classics on two jacks

VO The last QTP board in the archive looks back. The RTO-144 carries two RJ45 jacks, QTP-A and QTP-B, four balanced pairs on each, and a switch for 48 volt phantom power. A connection wizard maps the pins of a classic module, an M470A line in, mic in, insert and direct out, onto those eight pairs. In 2020 it is in a rack with two M460A input modules, Ward-Beck modules from 1974 on network cable.

6.1.8 Close: copper, one more time

VO While the preMO put the microphone on the network, QTP kept the twisted pair. It used the same cable as the network, but the signal on it was still analog. Both ideas came from the same company in the same few years.
