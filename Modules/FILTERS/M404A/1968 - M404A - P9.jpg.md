To gain access to the component boards and switch decks it is necessary to remove the metal side cover which is to the life side of the etched escutcheon This is accomplished by removing the two 2-56 flat-head screws on the left side of the M404A escutcheon and the two 4-40 round-head screws located on the bottom of the unit Do not loosen the 4-40 screw immediately adjacent to the M404A connector; this secures the frame ground lug internal to the equalizer
The PC boards may be examined by placing the equalizer on flat work area with the cover removed, the connector positioned to the left and with the control knobs positioned away from the inspector The amplifier and equalizer boards are located in position by retaining grooves fabricated from aluminum tabs spot-welded to the equalizer case Both PC boards may be extended by the length of the cable assembly by gently lifting the boards alternately out of the retaining grooves The boards may be supported by moving each assembly over to the right and temporarily resting the edge of the PC board on the metal enclosure Care should be exercised to prevent damage to the inter-connecting cable assemblies and upon reassembly, the flexible wires should be channeled under the PC board notch-outs to prevent crimping_
The location of components is shown in drawings 128-06-004 (amplifier) and 129-06-002 (equalizer) If fault is suspected, measuring the dc voltage present at the transistor electrodes may assist to determine the cause When compar ing the measurements listed in the voltage chart consideration should be given to the fact that, for direct coupled sections fault in one circuit may cause abnormal conditions in other circuit areas within the same section.
If it 15 necessary to interchange component to determine the cause of fault, first remove the solder from the printed circuit foil fillets associated with the suspected defective component. To do this, light soldering iron should be used (25 watts) in combination with an inexpensive vacuum action type hand desoldering tool. The desoldering tool is designed for component reworking and comprises teflon tip and cylinder equipped with spring plunger which, when loaded and released, creates high impulsive vacuum to draw the molten solder off from the printed circuit board and into the cylinder
