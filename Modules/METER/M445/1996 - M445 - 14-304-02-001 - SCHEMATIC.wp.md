The M445 Extended Range Meter/Monitor  combines two instruments in one: A rack-mounted frame and a portable extended range meter, for the measurement and monitoring of stereo analog audio signals.

When the M445 is docked in it's rack mounted frame, the sophisticated battery management circuitry places the battery in "quick-charge" while it assesses the battery condition. The circuitry continuously manages the battery while the meter is docked.

When docked; the M445 activates the meter illumination and speaker amplifiers, enabling confidence monitoring of the measured audio source.

An audio source may be permanently connected to the rack mounted assembly. This source is connected to the meter directly; whenever there are no plugs present in the M445 front panel long  frame jacks. Interruption of the audio connected to the rack chassis allows the M445 to bridge across an audio source on a day to day basis, while providing the ability to "patch in" a different audio feed.

A rotary encoder controls the precision attenuator integral to the M445. The liquid crystal display indicates the reference level indicated at the 0 VU position on the meters. The display also indicates whether or not the unit requires charging (by the "lo bat" indicator). If the battery voltage is too low for the M445 to operate accurately; the "low bat" will illuminate, but no digits will be visible in the display. This condition will persist until the meter is docked.

Pushbuttons within the M445 allow you to bridge or match the measured source. When placed in matching mode, either a 600 or 150 Ohm load is presented to the source.  Stereo phase integrity can be confirmed by placing the M445 into sum and difference mode. The sum of the left and right input signal is presented on the left-hand meter; and the algebraic difference is demonstrated on the right-hand meter.

The M445 contains a -18 dB per octave high-pass filter. When selected, this filter provides low frequency rejection of hum by -3dB at 75 Hz. The 1/4" standard TRS receptacle on the front panel is capable of driving a 600 ohm headset to a comfortable listening level. Additional monitoring of the measured audio may be done through the rear mounted terminal strip. This output may be used to drive an additional audio power amplifier.

The meter docking assembly is powered by an ac adapter, which furnishes 24 volts DC to the dock. This power is distributed to the speaker amplifiers, and to the meter (when docked). The dock; through it's rear-mounted barrier strip and docking connector, distributes all of the power and audio signals through the meter system. 

Two security features are standard. A front panel lock prevents the meter from being extracted when engaged. A second locking screw, accessible at the rear of the docking frame, provides a more permanent security lock.

1)	Left Channel VU Meter

Meter which indicates the volume units (in dBu) of the input audio signal. A sinusoidal waveform will indicate '0' on this meter when it's amplitude matches the sensitivity indicated on the liquid crystal display. Under normal operating conditions, this meter indicates the signal level of the left channel.

When "sum and difference" mode is selected by mode switch (3), this meter will display the algebraic sum of  the left and right channel audio signal.

2)	Right Channel VU Meter

Operates as (1), except in normal operation right channel audio is displayed. In "sum and difference" mode, this meter indicates the difference between the left and right channel audio. The difference indication is useful for verifying stereo phase coherence.

3)	Mode Switch

When depressed, places the M445 in "sum an difference" mode; otherwise discrete left and right signal levels are displayed on their associated meters.

4)	HP Filter Switch

Normally, the M445 measures the audio signal across the full audible band. When assigned, the high pass filter attenuates frequencies below it's 75 Hz corner at a rate of -18 dB per octave.

5)	Termination Switch

When released, the termination switch allows the meter to present a bridging load to the audio source. Once depressed, the impedance selected by the Impedance Selector (Z), is connected across the audio input terminals.

6)	Impedance  Selector

This switch terminates the input source with 600 ohms (relaxed) or 150 ohms (engaged): provided the termination switch has been actuated.

7)	Monitor Receptacle

A 1/4" standard TRS (tip-ring-sleeve) stereo receptacle for monitoring the metered audio signal. This jack provides a high quality, full bandwidth monitor output. Good quality, high impedance (>600 ohms) headphones will provide optimum audio fidelity.

8)	Left and Right Front Panel Inputs

Telephone style, balanced, long frame 1/4" jacks, which have the rear chassis input "normalled" through. When a plug (ADC PJ-2 or equivalent) is inserted, the rear chassis input audio is interrupted, allowing any audio present at the front panel to be measured and/or monitored.


9)	Power Switch

Powers the meter's active circuitry, exclusive of the battery management system. When docked; engaging the power switch also activates the meter illumination and the power amplifier which drives the speakers.

10)	Liquid Crystal Sensitivity Display

Controlled by (11), this display indicates the audio reference  level in dBu. When "lo batt" is displayed in the display, the batteries require charging; which is accomplished whenever the meter is "docked". Should the batteries deplete to a point where accurate measurements are no longer possible, the audio reference display will blank: However, the "lo batt" will still be visible.

11)	Sensitivity Adjustment Control

A rotary encoder which sets the reference level for a '0' indication on the meters of the M445.
12)	Security Latch

When engaged, this latch prevents the M445 from being extracted.

13)	Speaker Level Control

This rotary control adjusts the level of the audio in the monitor speakers.

14)	Right Channel Speaker

In "normal" mode, right channel audio may be monitored via this speaker. In "sum and difference"  mode, the difference signal will be present through this speaker.

15)	Left Channel Speaker

Discrete left channel audio is monitored in this speaker in "normal" mode, while in "sum and difference", this speaker contains the sum (L+R) signal.

UNPACKING AND INSTALLATION

M445 EXTENDED RANGE METER/MONITOR

Initial Inspection

The M445 carton should contain:

1 - This User's Manual
1 - ac adapter complete with connector.
1- M445 meter
1- Rackmount speaker assembly


Installation

The ac adapter, meter, and rack mount assembly should all be separated for installation.

1)	Place the rackmount assembly in your equipment rack. Finger tighten the four screws.

2)	Carefully dock the M445 in the rack assembly. It may be necessary to adjust the rack frame slightly so that the meter will insert and extract easily. Tighten the four rack screws while the meter is docked.

3)	At the rear of your rack, insert and lock the power connector. Connect your audio source and power amplifier (if needed) to the barrier strip and plug the unit in.

4)	The meter will operate when the power switch is engaged on the front panel.

Note: Before using the meter in portable mode, it should be left docked overnight to ensure that the batteries are fully exercised and charged.




Configuration Selection

The M445 is factory configured to return to +4 dBu reference sensitivity when initialized. Should your prefer 0 dBu or +8 dBu as the reference sensitivity, you can configure the M445 to reset to this state by changing two internal shorting clips.

Simply remove the top cover of the meter. To set a 0 dBu initial reference, remove the shorting clip across LK3, ensuring that the LK2 clip remains in place. For configuration to +8 dBu reference level, leave LK3 in place and remove LK2.

It is a good idea to save any shorting clips that you remove for possible future use.
