The location of components is shown in drawings 128-06-003 (amplifier) and 144-06-002 (equalizer) If fault is suspected, measuring the dc vol tage present at the transistor electrodes may assist to determine the cause When comparing the measurements listed in the voltage chart cons ideration should be given to the fact that, for direct coupled sections  fault in one circuit may cause abnormal conditions in other circuit areas within the same section
If it is necessary to interchange component to deterinine the cause of fault, first remove the solder from the printed circuit foil fillets associated with the suspected defective component To do this, light soldering iron should be used (25 watts) in combination with an inexpensive vacuum action type hand desoldering tool The desoldering tool is designed for component reworking and comprises teflon tip and cylinder equipped with spring plunger which, when loaded and released, creates high impulsive vacuum to draw the molten solder off froi the printed circuit board and into the cylinder
Place the soldering iron and the desoldering tool on the solder connection to be desoldered_ When the solder melts thoroughly, press the plunger release trigger Apply the solder ing iron on the connection until the vacuum stroke has been completed If any of the lead connections stick to the printed circuit board, free them with scribe or similar sharply pointed instrument Carefully remove the component which, if proven to be satisfactory, may be used again for other applications
To install the new   component, shape the leads to the correct spacing _ Insert the part and fold the leads under the printed circuit board to firmly hold the component Clip off the excess wire leaving about 1/16 inch _ Solder the new leads _ using resin core solder and clean the board with an inert fluid such as perchlorethelyne
