Episode 1 · 1960|-7 – 1972|5 · 119 slides

A Sound Idea

Two men in a garage, a broadcaster with a problem nobody's catalogue could solve, and the modular building blocks of the 100, 400 and 600 series, plus the power supplies that ran them.

Episode 2 · 1972|5 – 1989|22 · 120 slides

The Console Years

From the 440 channel amps to the 450 summing family, the 460 series and the M461 transportables, the 470 L-series, the 490 stereo cards, microCOM, and the 500 series that came and went.

Episode 2.1 · 1968|1 – 1979|12 · 110 slides

Turn the Knob: Filters and Equalizers

From the stepped M402 effects equalizer and the M404 hi-lo filter of 1968, through the A and B rework, to the 1974 M462 state variable equalizer, the M464 filter and the M472 channel equalizer: how Ward-Beck's equalizers became parametric.

Episode 2.2 · 1968|1 – 1988|21 · 120 slides

The Preamp: Building Blocks

One amplifier card in 1968, and twenty years of input channels built from the same parts: the M124, the M600, the M441, the M460 and its plug-in boards, the M462 that shares its filters, the M470, the M490 and the M520.

Episode 2.3 · 1968|1 – 1995|28 · 112 slides

The Needle That Falls

Compression, limiting and de-essing from the M406 to the M466, and the narrow edgewise meter in the M466M and M467 that showed the operator how much the compressor was taking away.

Episode 3 · 1970|3 – 1999|32 · 111 slides

Talkback: The Intercom Story

Ward-Beck's first big job was intercom, and so was much of its last decade. Crosspoints, the 7000 series, microCOM and microCOM II, turrets, IFB and Minicom: the matrix that let a director talk into the right ear.

Episode 3.1 · 1988|21 – 1993|26 · 43 slides

The MicroCOM Control Story

How the MicroCOM II was told what to do: a DOS program on an IBM-type PC, four small files, a twelve-hundred-baud cable, thirty-five-byte blocks, and a frame full of microcontrollers that never needed the computer once the show was on.

Episode 4 · 1971|4 – 2000|33 · 111 slides

On Air: The Radio Console

A radio console is an intercom that mutes signals to a bus. From CKEY in 1971 to the R-series, R2K and Renaissance MK I–III: the mute bus, the tally, the turrets, the 900-series cards, and a decade of radio groups and the CBC.

Episode 5 · 2000|33 – 2025|58 · 95 slides

Third Generation and Beyond

Act 1, 2000–2010: the consoles become collectibles and the company changes shape. Act 2, 2010 onward: metering, the PODs, openGear cards, and the consoles' afterlives with their owners, ending with WILMA.

Episode 5.5 · 2008|41 – 2015|48 · 37 slides

The openGear Partnership

A metal box before a card. Ward-Beck did the mechanical and electro-mechanical work on the shielding enclosure that became the first openGear frame, then filled it: audio and video DAs, AES converters, SDI embedders and a Dolby E decoder, all built to an open standard.

Episode 6 · 2013|46 – 2021|54 · 51 slides

Ten Years Early: preMO and the Network

Mic in, multicast out. From the first RAVENNA and AES67 meters to the preMO-A1 network microphone preamp at NAB 2017 and the preMO-221 and 222 in 2021: Ward-Beck put the microphone on the network a decade before the industry caught up.

Episode 6.1 · 2015|48 – 2020|53 · 28 slides

Quad Twisted Pair

Four balanced pairs in one jacket. QTP was Ward-Beck's series of cabling and connecting products: RJ45 and DB25 boards, a QTP version of the POD8A, an XLR encoder, a patchbay, and the RTO-144 that puts a classic Ward-Beck module on two RJ45 jacks.

Episode 7 · 2003|36 – 2026|59 · 115 slides

The WBSps: Preservation Society

In 2003 a twenty-year-old in Saskatoon moved a Ward-Beck console out of CBC Regina and started a website to find its manuals. Twenty-three years later that site is WBSps.ca and WBSps.Kuzub.com: a sorted archive of almost 15,000 files. The story of the Ward-Beck Systems Preservation Society, and of what people did after the consoles.
