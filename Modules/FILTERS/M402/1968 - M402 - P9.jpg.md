### Page 1 (OCR, spell-checked: 1 words corrected)

```text
The location of components is shown in drawings 28-06-002 (amplifier) and 144-06-001 (equalizer). If fault is suspected, measuring the dc voltage present at the transistor electrodes may assist to determine the cause. When comparing the measurements listed in the voltage chart consideration should be given to the fact that, for direct coupled sections, a fault in one circuit may cause abnormal conditions in other circuit areas within the same section.
If it is necessary to interchange a component to determine the cause of a fault, first remove the solder from the printed circuit foil fillets associated with the suspected defective component_ To do this, light soldering iron should be used (25 watts) in combination with an inexpensive vacuum action type hand desoldering tool _ The desoldering tool is designed for component reworking &nd comprises a teflon tip and cylinder equipped with spring plunger which, when loaded and released creates high impulsive vacuum to draw the molten solder off from the printed circuit board and into the cylinder_
Place the soldering iron and the desoldering tool on the solder connection to be desoldered. When the solder melts thoroughly, press the plunger release trigger Apply the soldering iron on the connection until the vacuum stroke has been completed. If any of the lead connections stick to the printed circuit board, free them with a scribe or a similar sharply pointed instrument. Carefully remove the component which, if proven to be satisfactory, may be Used again for other applications
To install the new component, shape the leads to the correct spacing. Insert the part and fold the leads under the printed circuit board to firmly hold the component. Clip off the excess wire leaving about 1/16 inch. Solder the new leads, using resin core solder and clean the board with an inert fluid such as perchlorethelyne.
```
